## 2. Regulatory constraints

### Canada (if the product serves Canadian applications)
- Bill C-35 ("Cracking Down on Crooked Consultants Act") made charging money for immigration advice by unauthorized individuals a criminal offence. Fines range from $20,000 to $100,000 per secondary summary. Lawyers, Quebec notaries, and unpaid third parties are excluded.
- Authorized practitioners named in a December 2024 Canada Gazette Part I notice: members of the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants (RCIC), provincial or territorial law societies, and the Chambre des notaires du Québec.
- Unverified: whether AI-generated guidance counts as "advice for remuneration". No source found addressed AI tools directly.
- Unverified: new rules said to take effect 15 July 2026. Contents not retrieved.

### United States
- Federal baseline: immigration legal advice and representation are limited to licensed attorneys and DOJ-accredited representatives at recognized organizations. Non-lawyer "notarios" and visa consultants are not authorized to represent parties before DOJ immigration bodies.
- Form-filling carve-out: federal regulations exclude clerical help filling in blank spaces on DHS forms, for little or no pay, without holding oneself out as qualified in immigration matters. Advising on how to answer form questions is not clerical.
- State rules differ. Washington's Immigration Services Fraud Prevention Act is one example: it bans unauthorized immigration legal work, bars referral fees, and bars advising on form answers, while permitting translation.
- Sources are secondary (bar pages, firm blogs, a 2023 USCIS webinar, an older INS memo). Confirm against 8 CFR 292 and the target state statute before any launch.

### EU / Schengen

### Schengen short-stay, 2025 (secondary reports; Commission dataset not retrieved)
- About 12 million applications lodged, more than 10 million visas issued. Global refusal rate reported between 14.6% and 14.8%.
- Highest reported refusals: Bangladesh (~54.5%), Burundi (~53%), Senegal (~52%), Nigeria (~48%), Pakistan (~46%), Ghana (~46.5%), DR Congo (>40%).
- Lowest: China (~4%), Saudi Arabia (~6%), Russia (~6.4%).
- Consulate-level figures may be 2024 data. Sources disagree on several numbers. Do not publish any figure before checking the Commission's own dataset.

### EU entry systems
- EES (Entry/Exit System) is fully operational since 10 April 2026, with uneven performance at border crossings.
- ETIAS has no confirmed launch date. The Q4 2026 target was dropped by July 2026, and 2027 is now the likely start, pending a revised timeline expected in September 2026. Re-check before building any ETIAS feature.
